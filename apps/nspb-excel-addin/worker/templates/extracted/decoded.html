§ 01

Executive summary

The headline movement, where it comes from, and why it matters.

The analysis reveals a significant projected jump in profitability. Gross Profit is forecast to grow from $1.56M in FY24 to $4.42M in FY25 — a near 3× expansion of the bottom of the funnel.

Revenue is the primary driver of this growth, with a steady upward trend across every forecast month. Operating expenses tell a different story: FY24 spend was concentrated heavily in Selling Expenses ($1.04M, 71.2% of OpEx), and the FY25 plan projects a sharp reset of that line.

Read together, the plan is a bet on operating leverage: hold revenue's upward trajectory while compressing the variable-cost layer that fuelled FY24's go-to-market.

§ 06

Opportunities & risks

What the plan unlocks, and what it depends on.

Opportunities

  • Operating-leverage compounding If revenue lands and selling-cost reset holds, contribution margin re-rates structurally — the $1.15M GP gain is mostly margin, not volume.
  • Smoother monthly cadence FY25 forecast eliminates the FY24 swing between $191K and $486K, easing cash-flow planning and freeing working capital for reinvestment.
  • Training spend rebalanced A 24% reduction in training while go-to-market reorganises is directionally consistent — opportunity to redeploy that budget into capability building.

Risks & watch-outs

  • Commission cliff The 92% drop in commission ($943K → $77K) presumes a successful renegotiation of seller compensation. If sales motion is unchanged, this is a planning fiction.
  • COGS = $0 in Jan FY25 The forecast shows zero cost of goods sold in the first month of FY25. Either a structural shift in product mix, or — more likely — an open data input that needs validation.
  • Smoothed forecast curve The narrow $309–397K band suggests top-down planning rather than a bottom-up build; seasonality and one-offs may be under-modelled.
  • Automobile creep The only line trending up (+1.2%). Small in absolute terms, but worth confirming it reflects fleet additions rather than uncontrolled reimbursements.
§ 07

Recommended next steps

Prioritised by materiality to the FY25 plan.

Validate the commission re-base

Drill into Total Selling Expenses to surface what underlies the forecast 82% reduction. The Commission Expense drop from $943K to $77K is the single largest swing in the plan and deserves a documented assumption trail.

OwnerFP&A · Sales Ops

Pressure-test the revenue forecast

Confirm the volume and price assumptions that drive the +73.9% Gross Profit growth. Stress-test against historical attainment, pipeline coverage, and seasonality before committing the plan to the board.

OwnerFinance · Revenue

Resolve the Jan FY25 COGS anomaly

FY24 January COGS was $59K; the FY25 forecast for the same month is $0. Confirm whether this reflects a real product-mix shift or an unfilled data cell. A correction here flows through to monthly margin shape.

OwnerAccounting

Move from smoothed to bottom-up monthly forecast

The current FY25 monthly band ($309–397K) is unusually tight given FY24 volatility. Re-build the forecast bottom-up by month, layering known seasonality and one-off events, to give finance a defensible variance baseline.
